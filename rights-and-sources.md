# Rights and sources

The six poems and their historical edition notes are preserved in orb.json and in the reader. These eighteenth- and nineteenth-century texts are in the U.S. public domain. Original commentary and guided prose accompany them; the revised guided text is unrecorded. Older versioned scripts are retained as prior editions.

## Poem sources

- John Keats, To Autumn: [text and edition](https://poets.org/poem/autumn). Text as presented by the Academy of American Poets; written September 19, 1819, first published 1820. Three stanzas of eleven lines.
- Emily Dickinson, Autumn: [text and edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/12242). Early published text: Poems (1890), “Nature,” XXVIII, editorially titled “Autumn”; reproduced from the collected early-series transcription. This is not the modern Franklin reading text.
- Paul Laurence Dunbar, Merry Autumn: [text and edition](https://poets.org/poem/merry-autumn/print). Lyrics of Lowly Life (1896), as presented and identified as public domain by the Academy of American Poets. Ten quatrains.
- Helen Hunt Jackson, October: [text and edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9825/9825-h/9825-h.htm). “October” from A Calendar of Sonnets. The consulted 1891 printing bears an 1886 copyright date; this is not asserted to be the poem’s first periodical appearance.
- Robert Louis Stevenson, Autumn Fires: [text and edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25609). From A Child’s Garden of Verses (first published 1885), using the text of the 1905 illustrated edition. Three quatrains; illustrations from that edition are not reproduced here.
- William Blake, To Autumn: [text and edition](https://poets.org/poem/autumn-1). From Poetical Sketches (1783); text as presented by the Academy of American Poets. Three six-line stanzas. Apostrophe shapes are typographically normalized.

The historical artworks linked in the reader are separate references, not reproduced photographs or asserted influences. The walk is imaginary; regional photographs do not place these poets in the Pacific Northwest.
